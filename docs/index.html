Set up the electronics

Install the firmware, connect the display and check synchronization before fitting the other parts into the enclosure.

Install the firmware

  1. Use a standard 30-pin ESP32-WROOM with 4 MB of flash and a USB data cable. Check the pin labels on your board. View the wiring diagram.
  2. Download and extract the ESP32 archive from GitHub Releases. On Windows, install Python, then run python -m pip install esptool==5.1.0. Close the serial monitor before flashing.
  3. Run the command below from the extracted folder, replacing COM5 with your actual port. For an update that uses the same partition layout, omit -InitialInstall.
./flash-clock.ps1 -Port COM5 -InitialInstall

If a release is unavailable, open esp32/clockinator/clockinator.ino in the Arduino IDE. Install ESP32 board support and the ArduinoJson 7, GxEPD2 and Adafruit GFX libraries. Select your board and serial port, choose No OTA (2MB APP/2MB SPIFFS), then upload. Firmware guide.

Install the Android app

Download the APK from GitHub Releases and open it on Android. Allow installation from that source if prompted, then grant Bluetooth access when you open the app. Build the app in Android Studio if an APK is unavailable.

Sync for the first time

  1. Reconnect USB and keep the phone nearby. Scan in the setup wizard or under Clock and select your alarm clock. Its default Bluetooth name is Clockinator.
  2. Once connected, use Sync alarms on the connection screen if needed. Check that the display shows the time from your phone.
  3. The app syncs again when Bluetooth reconnects. Android may delay background reconnection. After a power cut, reconnect the phone to restore the time and routines.

For synchronization over your local network, follow the Wi-Fi tutorial.

Print and prepare the enclosure

Download the STL files and print at 100% scale. Print the body flat and the lid on its edge. Check the slicer preview to make sure the interior, the open light outlet and the nut pockets remain accessible.

  1. If support material remains in a nut pocket, place the part on a stable surface and carefully score the upper and lower attachments with a craft knife. Pull fragments out sideways with a toothpick or paper clip. A wooden skewer through the screw hole can help free them.
  2. Support the mounting post with a finger when scraping with a blunt tool. Avoid prying against its edges. If a post breaks, check that the lid can still be secured before proceeding.
  3. Fit the four M3 lid nuts in their side pockets and check screw alignment before adding wires.
  4. If light shines through the white lid, apply opaque tape to the top of the chamber and around the LED pocket. Keep the outlet toward the display clear. Opaque nonconductive tape can also cover the ESP32 power LED.

Prepare the RGB LED

  1. Identify the common lead from the LED pinout. The firmware expects a common cathode connected to GND. Check the pinout before relying on lead length.
  2. With USB unplugged, solder a 330 Ω resistor in series with each color lead. Leave the cathode accessible for the ground splice.
  3. Connect the red, green and blue control wires to GPIO13, GPIO12 and GPIO14. Temporarily connect the common lead to GND. Power the clock, connect the app, open Clock → Clock light and turn on the light. Test each color by briefly touching its wire to the resistor lead while keeping the other leads clear.
  4. Unplug USB, solder the three wires to the corresponding resistors and insulate the joints. Connect the cathode to the shared ground for the display and buttons. Support the leads at the base when bending them 90° and check that the LED fits in the lid. Leave it out until final assembly.

Prepare the speaker

  1. Connect the speaker to SPK+ and SPK− on the MAX98357A. Use the screw terminal or solder the wires, depending on your module.
  2. Connect VIN to VIN/5V and GND to GND on the ESP32. This setup assumes the board provides USB 5 V on VIN.
  3. Connect LRC / WS to GPIO25, BCLK to GPIO26 and DIN to GPIO27.
  4. With the app connected, open Clock → Clock ringtone volume → Play on clock · 5 seconds. Start at a low volume and check the speaker.

Set the maximum ringing time under Clock → Automatic stop. The default is 20 minutes. The ESP32 saves the setting and stops the alarm even when the phone is disconnected.

To upload your own sound, follow the ringtone tutorial.

Wire and test the buttons

  1. Prepare two flexible wires per KS-33 switch, one for signal and one for GND. Start with 5–10 cm and leave enough slack to lift the switch plate.
  2. With USB unplugged, tin the wires and solder them to the two metal pins. Feed the wires through the opening before clipping each switch into place. The keycaps from smallest to largest are Light, Stop and Snooze.
  3. Splice the three GND wires together with a pigtail and insulate the joint with heat-shrink tubing. Join the pigtail to the LED cathode and display GND, then connect the splice to an ESP32 GND pin. The amplifier uses the other GND pin.
  4. Connect Light to GPIO21, Stop to GPIO32 and Snooze to GPIO33. The firmware enables the internal pull-up on all three.
  5. Power on the clock and press Light twice. To test Stop and Snooze, schedule a routine a few minutes ahead, sync, then test both buttons when the alarm rings.

Assemble the clock

  1. Unplug USB, check all connections and untangle the wires. Route them toward the center and clear of the lid guides and screw posts.
  2. Fasten the speaker with four M3 × 8 mm screws and four M3 nuts. Insert the screws from outside the case. Start with the two most accessible mounting points. Long straight pliers can help hold the other nuts. Tighten gently to protect the thin wall.
  3. Fasten the MAX98357A with two M2 × 20 mm screws and two M2 nuts for this assembly. Insert the nuts from underneath. Check that the threads pass through the nuts before tightening. The CAD bill of materials gives a different length for a different stack-up.
  4. Slide the display into its guides. Leave the LED out on the display side and check the cable routing.
  5. Fasten the ESP32 with four M3 × 25 mm screws from above and four M3 nuts underneath. Check that the USB-C cable fits.
  6. Check that all four lid nuts line up. Fit the switch plate and keycaps, insert the LED, then lower the lid gently. If the lid catches, check the wires, display and guides.
  7. Secure the lid with four M3 × 25 mm screws and check that all three keys return freely.

Finish and test

  1. Reconnect USB and test the display, speaker, buttons and light again. Check the lighting in the dark.
  2. Once the LED is positioned correctly, secure it with a small dab of hot glue or a piece of tape so you can remove it later.
  3. Stick the feet into the recesses under the enclosure. Test a scheduled alarm, then restart the clock and sync it again.
